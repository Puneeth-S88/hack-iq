# HackIQ — Full Question Bank (All 7 Rounds, Revised)

Beginner-friendly for all years. Correct answers marked **✔**. Rounds 1–4 are auto-gradable (A/B/C/D). Round 5 is a live pitch round. Round 6 mixes auto-graded questions with a live jury-explain segment. Round 7 is a live multi-stage decision simulation.

## ROUND 1 — MCQ Warmup (10 Questions, 1 point each) — All Teams

1. What makes a password strong?
 A) Your pet's name B) A mix of upper/lower case, numbers, and symbols ✔ C) Your birth year D) The word "password123"
 **Correct: B**

2. What does 2FA stand for?
 A) Two-Factor Authentication ✔ B) Two-File Access C) Firewall Authorization D) Fast Access
 **Correct: A**

3. Which of these is the safest password?
 A) `123456` B) `qwerty` C) `Tr@ck!ng_Sunset92` ✔ D) `password`
 **Correct: C**

4. Is it safe to log into your bank account on public airport WiFi?
 A) Yes, always B) Only if the WiFi has a password C) No, avoid it or use a VPN ✔ D) Yes, if the site has "http"
 **Correct: C**

5. Which of these is a sign of a phishing email?
 A) Comes from a known colleague B) Creates urgency and asks you to click a link immediately ✔ C) Has no attachments D) Is well-formatted
 **Correct: B**

6. What is "shoulder surfing"?
 A) A type of malware B) Someone watching you enter a password/PIN in person ✔ C) A social media trend D) A firewall setting
 **Correct: B**

7. Which is the best practice for passwords across different accounts?
 A) Use the same password everywhere for convenience B) Use a unique password for each account ✔ C) Write all passwords on a sticky note D) Share passwords with close friends
 **Correct: B**

8. What does HTTPS in a website URL indicate?
 A) The site is definitely safe B) The connection is encrypted ✔ C) The site is government-owned D) The site has no ads
 **Correct: B**

9. What is a common sign of a scam call/message?
 A) It thanks you for being a loyal customer B) It asks for OTP or bank details urgently ✔ C) It's from a saved contact D) It has correct grammar
 **Correct: B**

10. What is "phishing"?
 A) A method of catching fish online B) Tricking someone into revealing sensitive info via fake communication ✔ C) A type of firewall D) An antivirus feature
 **Correct: B**

## ROUND 3 — Spot the Phishing (10 Items, 2 points each) — All Teams

*Render each as a styled "message card" (email/SMS/login page look) rather than plain text.*

1. **Email from:** `support@paypa1-secure.com` — "Your account has been LIMITED — verify within 24 hours!" Link: `bit.ly/paypal-verify-now`.
 A) Genuine B) Phishing — misspelled domain, urgency, shortened link ✔ C) Genuine, has PayPal logo D) Can't tell
 **Correct: B**

2. **SMS from "BANK":** "Your a/c will be blocked today. Update KYC immediately: hxxp://kyc-verify-bank.ml/update"
 A) Genuine B) Phishing — suspicious domain, urgency, generic greeting ✔ C) Genuine, banks send SMS often D) Can't tell
 **Correct: B**

3. **Email from:** `hr@yourcompany.com` — "Updated Leave Policy — attached PDF, review by Friday." No links, real HR name.
 A) Genuine — internal domain, no urgency, no suspicious link ✔ B) Phishing C) Genuine only because of the PDF D) Can't tell
 **Correct: A**

4. **Login page** that looks exactly like Instagram, URL: `instagram-login-secure.net`.
 A) Genuine B) Phishing — fake domain hosting a cloned login page ✔ C) Genuine, Instagram uses many domains D) Can't tell
 **Correct: B**

5. **Email from:** `no-reply@netflix.com` — "Your payment failed," professional tone, link goes to `netflix.com/account/billing`.
 A) Genuine — real domain, professional tone, no pressure ✔ B) Phishing, all payment emails are scams C) Genuine only because of logo D) Can't tell
 **Correct: A**

6. **WhatsApp** from unknown number: "Hi, I'm from your college placement cell. Register here: `tinyurl.com/placement-2026-urgent`"
 A) Genuine B) Phishing — unknown number, urgency, shortened link, unofficial channel ✔ C) Genuine, mentions college D) Can't tell
 **Correct: B**

7. **Email from:** `security@google.com` (verified) — "New sign-in detected from Chennai. If not you, secure your account." Link: `myaccount.google.com`.
 A) Genuine — verified domain, real format, link matches ✔ B) Phishing, all such emails are fake C) Genuine only if you're in Chennai D) Can't tell
 **Correct: A**

8. **Email:** "CONGRATULATIONS!!! You WON an iPhone 16!! Claim before it EXPIRES in 10 mins!! Pay ₹99 shipping with your card."
 A) Genuine B) Phishing — urgency, unearned prize, asks for card details ✔ C) Genuine, only the timer is fake D) Can't tell
 **Correct: B**

9. **Delivery SMS:** "Your parcel is on hold due to unpaid customs fee of ₹49. Pay here: `delivery-fee-pay.xyz`"
 A) Genuine, couriers do this B) Phishing — odd domain, small urgent payment request, unsolicited ✔ C) Genuine if you're expecting a parcel D) Can't tell
 **Correct: B**

10. **LinkedIn message** from a real-looking recruiter profile, no links, asking to schedule a call via LinkedIn itself to discuss an internship.
 A) Genuine — normal recruiting behavior, no red flags ✔ B) Phishing, all unsolicited messages are scams C) Genuine only if you already applied D) Can't tell
 **Correct: A**

## ROUND 4 — Decode the Message (10 Ciphers, 3 points each) — All Teams

1. **Caesar +3:** `VWDB DOHUW`
 A) STAY ALERT ✔ B) STAY AWAKE C) BE ALERT D) ACT SMART
 **Correct: A**

2. **ROT13:** `OR FNSR`
 A) BE SAFE ✔ B) BE SURE C) BE SMART D) BE SAFE NOW
 **Correct: A**

3. **Caesar +5:** `YMNSP YBNHJ`
 A) THINK TWICE ✔ B) THINK ONCE C) ACT TWICE D) THINK FAST
 **Correct: A**

4. **Atbash (A=Z, B=Y, C=X...):** `KZHHDLIW HZUVGB`
 A) PASSWORD SAFETY ✔ B) PASSWORD SECURE C) PROTECT SAFETY D) PASSCODE SAFETY
 **Correct: A**

5. **ROT13:** `ARIRE FUNER BGC`
 A) NEVER SHARE OTP ✔ B) ALWAYS SHARE OTP C) NEVER TRUST OTP D) NEVER FORGET OTP
 **Correct: A**

6. **Caesar +2:** `NQEM AQWT UETGGP`
 A) LOCK YOUR SCREEN ✔ B) LOCK YOUR PHONE C) HIDE YOUR SCREEN D) CHECK YOUR SCREEN
 **Correct: A**

7. **ROT13:** `GEHFG AB YVAX`
 A) TRUST NO LINK ✔ B) CLICK NO LINK C) TRUST ALL LINKS D) AVOID ALL LINKS
 **Correct: A**

8. **Caesar +4:** `WXEC WLEVT`
 A) STAY SHARP ✔ B) STAY SAFE C) BE SHARP D) ACT SHARP
 **Correct: A**

9. **Atbash:** `GIFHG YFG EVIRUB`
 A) TRUST BUT VERIFY ✔ B) TRUST AND VERIFY C) DOUBT AND VERIFY D) TRUST BUT IGNORE
 **Correct: A**

10. **Caesar +6:** `INKIQ ZNK YKTJKX`
 A) CHECK THE SENDER ✔ B) CHECK THE SUBJECT C) TRUST THE SENDER D) BLOCK THE SENDER
 **Correct: A**

*Answer key note for organizers: to encode, shift each letter forward by N; to decode, shift back by N. ROT13 is self-inverse (apply twice to get original). Atbash: A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X, D↔W, E↔V, F↔U, G↔T, H↔S, I↔R, J↔Q, K↔P, L↔O, M↔N.*

## ROUND 5 — Case Study Pitch (10 Case Studies) — All Teams, Live Judging

**Format:** Each team draws one case study (repeat cases if fewer than 10 teams, or if more teams, pair two teams per case for a live head-to-head pitch). Give teams 2 minutes to discuss, then 2 minutes to pitch their solution to the jury. No slides needed — verbal + optional whiteboard sketch.

**Judging rubric (10 points per team):**
- Understood the actual risk (0-4 pts)
- Solution is practical and realistic (0-4 pts)
- Clearly communicated within time (0-2 pts)

**The 10 Cases:**

1. A college club's Instagram account got hacked and is now posting spam to followers. What should they do to recover it and prevent a repeat?
2. A student received a "job offer" asking for an advance payment to "confirm the seat." Design a 3-step way to warn other students about this.
3. Your college WiFi has no login or restrictions — anyone nearby can connect. Propose a simple policy to make it safer.
4. A friend's phone was stolen. It had banking apps installed and no lock screen. What should they do in the first hour, and in the following days?
5. Fake QR codes were pasted over the real ones at the canteen counter, redirecting payments to a scammer. How would you help the canteen prevent this?
6. An older relative keeps forwarding "you've won a prize" WhatsApp messages. Design a simple 3-step way to help them spot scams themselves.
7. Your college is building an online fest registration form that collects student personal data. What privacy/security practices should the organizers follow?
8. A teammate accidentally shared the hostel WiFi password in a public group chat with 500+ unknown members. What should be done immediately, and going forward?
9. Someone clicked a link in an email that looked like it was from the IT department, and their laptop is now acting strange (pop-ups, slow, odd files). Walk through what should happen next.
10. A local shop owner wants to start accepting UPI payments and is worried about fraud. Suggest simple precautions.

## ROUND 6 — Semifinal Challenge (Top 5 Teams Only)

**Part A — Speed Round (10 pts total, auto-graded, ~6 min):**

*5 MCQs (2 pts each):*

1. What is "spear phishing"? A) Phishing targeted at a specific person using info about them ✔ B) Phishing sent using emojis C) A firewall technique D) A type of antivirus — **Correct: A**
2. Safest way to verify a bank's request: A) Call the number given in their message B) Call the official number on your card/bank's website ✔ C) Reply to the SMS D) Trust it if it has the bank's logo — **Correct: B**
3. Common giveaway of a fake login page: A) The URL doesn't exactly match the real site's domain ✔ B) The page looks colorful C) The page loads fast D) It has a login button — **Correct: A**
4. A "friend" urgently messages asking for money for an emergency. Best first step? A) Send money immediately B) Verify by calling them directly on a known number ✔ C) Ask for their bank details first D) Ignore forever — **Correct: B**
5. Why is reusing passwords across accounts risky? A) Slower login B) A breach in one account can compromise all linked accounts ✔ C) Websites block it anyway D) It isn't risky if the password is long — **Correct: B**

*5 True/False (1 pt each):*

6. A padlock icon in the browser always means the site is 100% legitimate. → **False**
7. Two-factor authentication can still be bypassed if you're tricked into approving a fake prompt. → **True**
8. Deleting a phishing email is enough — no need to report it. → **False**
9. Attackers often research a target's social media before sending a targeted phishing message. → **True**
10. It's fine to reuse your college email password for personal social media accounts. → **False**

**Part B — Explain to the Jury (15 pts, live, ~2 min per team):**

Each of the 5 teams is handed a *different* mock item and must explain to the jury panel: what's suspicious about it, why it's risky, and what they'd actually do. Judged on accuracy, reasoning, and clarity (not just a right/wrong answer).

- **Team 1:** A mock email — correct sender name but wrong domain, urgent tone, generic greeting.
- **Team 2:** Side-by-side screenshots of a real login page and a cloned fake one.
- **Team 3:** A short incident description — a laptop shows a pop-up demanding payment to "unlock" files.
- **Team 4:** A WhatsApp forward about a "government scheme" offering free money via a link.
- **Team 5:** A scenario where a shop's QR code was swapped, redirecting customer payments elsewhere.

Top 3 by combined Part A + Part B score advance to the Final.

## ROUND 7 — Final Showdown: The Live Breach Room (Top 3 Teams)

**Format:** A single unfolding incident, read out by the jury/host in 5 stages. After each stage, all 3 teams write their decision (A/B/C/D or a short line) on a whiteboard/paper simultaneously within 30 seconds, then reveal together. No conferring between teams. This is the most visible, crowd-facing round — run it on stage with the story narrated dramatically.

**Scenario: "The College Portal Under Attack"**

**Stage 1 (4 pts):** Students report they can't log in — they're seeing a page that looks like the portal but feels slightly off. What does your team do first?
A) Ignore it, wait for IT B) Immediately alert IT/admin and warn students not to enter credentials ✔ C) Post about it publicly on social media D) Try logging in yourself to test it
**Correct: B**

**Stage 2 (4 pts):** IT confirms it's a fake page mimicking the real portal. Some students already entered their passwords. What's next?
A) Nothing, it's IT's problem now B) Advise affected students to change passwords immediately and enable 2FA ✔ C) Blame the students publicly D) Shut down all internet access college-wide
**Correct: B**

**Stage 3 (4 pts):** The attacker sends a mass email posing as "college admin," asking everyone to click a "security patch" link. What do you do?
A) Forward the email to everyone B) Send an official warning through verified channels not to click, and report/block the sender ✔ C) Wait and see if anyone complains D) Click it yourself to check
**Correct: B**

**Stage 4 (4 pts):** A journalist calls asking about the "hack" for a story. How do you respond?
A) Share every technical detail immediately B) Direct them to the official college communications/IT team ✔ C) Deny anything happened D) Give personal opinions on the record
**Correct: B**

**Stage 5 — Final Tiebreaker (10 pts, jury-judged, open-ended):** In one sentence, what's the single most important lesson from this whole incident to prevent it happening again? Each team answers aloud; jury scores for insight and clarity — this is what decides ties and finalizes the overall 1st/2nd/3rd ranking.
